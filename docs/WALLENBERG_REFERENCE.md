# Wallenberg four block reference

## Sources inspected

- [Jenkem, *We charted some of skateboarding's most iconic spots* (27 July 2017)](https://www.jenkemmag.com/home/2017/07/27/charted-skateboardings-iconic-spots/). The magazine collected spot measurements from readers. Its Wallenberg entry gives **51 inches high, 198 inches long**. Those inch values are authoritative for this reconstruction; the page rounds 51 inches to 4.3 feet, and a widely shared comparison chart rounds 198 inches (5.03 m) to "16 feet".
- Photographs the user supplied (October 2026): the 51-inch measurement shot from the street, looking up the blocks with the court-side slope on the right; a clip still from the court side at the bottom, showing the side stairs and the gym's door; and two stills from *Wallenberg – Skate History* of a rider dropping in on the plywood roll-in and rolling out along the gym's side wall.
- [FindSkateSpots, Wallenberg 4](https://findskatespots.com/spots/sf-ca/vega-street-wallenberg-4), [photograph inspected](https://images.findskatespots.com/images/large/8a4d5975c64090456b312c9aa5a9c852.jpg). From the bottom by the side stairs: the gym's front with its door and blue awning, brick base toward its corner, blue panels and round vents; the side stairs in flights of three with galvanized rails; the yucca at their foot.
- [Jenkem, *A new generation's take on Wallenberg* (14 November 2024)](https://www.jenkemmag.com/home/2024/11/14/a-new-generations-take-on-wallenberg/), photographs by Bailey Schreiner; the [group photograph](https://cdn.jenkemmag.com/mediaAssetsMaster/2024/11/GROUPWALLENBERGPHOTO.png) shows the roll-in standing in the alley with brick behind it, the gym to its left; the trick photographs show the blocks' rounded ends from the court side.
- The user's [Ryan Ferguson overview](https://ryanferguson.co.uk/blogs/blog/sacred-skate-spots-skateboarding-world) served as the discovery starting point.

## Geometry decisions

The world uses 29 units per foot (`SET_FOOT`), matching El Toro; the stage scales it onto the riders' units with `SET_SCALE`. The first launch lip is `x=0, y=0`; positive x travels down the gap, and looking up the blocks from the street +z is on the left. Photographs fix the handedness: the side stairs and the gym are on the left, the slope on the right.

**The four curbs.** 51 inches fall over 198 inches lip to lip, as three 15-inch blocks and the short 6-inch street curb at the bottom (the photographs show the bottom curb at well under half the others; individual heights were not surveyed). Treads are equal, 5.5 feet, dark weathered asphalt between white painted faces with worn caps. The side stairs beside their square ends come down in flights of three steps per block, one step at the street curb, with galvanized rails along both sides.

**The court side.** The lower two curbs run on out of sight. The top two end in rounded corners, the second reaching further out, and beyond them the ground is one broad asphalt slope: level with the second tread where the second curb's face would run, rising back at a 13% grade past both rounded ends, whose side faces sink into it, to a chain-link fence and double gate, with apartments beyond. Along the alley the slope is held back by a low concrete ledge with a pipe rail on top, about 2.6 feet high at the back.

**The run-up.** Behind the 8-foot top platform the gym stands with its corner on the line: its front with the door and blue awning faces the gap, and its side wall (brick base, round vents, lights, blue panels) runs back along the alley the rider rolls down. The plywood roll-in stands on its own framing in the alley, its high end set against the little roof over a door in the gym's side wall, level with its 8-foot deck: a rounded lip and a transition steepening to 65°. A one-storey brick annex with a pink door and a planter on its roof stands well behind it, against the gym's side wall near its back.

**The drop-in.** The rider starts on the deck with a nudge and drops in, gaining speed down the transition under gravity, less rolling and air losses, so they reach the speed the gap takes at its foot (about 21 ft/s for a neutral pop, close to what eight frictionless feet give). They ride square to the plywood, both trucks on it, then roll the 17 feet of alley and platform to the lip; the trick's crouch starts on the flat. `DropTerrain.dropIn` in `sets/elToro/stairs.ts` plans it.

**Cameras.** The filmers stand where the photographs were taken: on the street straight up the line (the Dream Tricks classic), on the court side below the rounded ends, and beside the lip looking up the alley. The crane can't stand inside the gym: down the alley it swings round to film along the gym's wall, ahead of or behind the rider, and swings back out once they're past the corner, no faster than 2.8° per foot they travel. Over the roll-in and the annex it tilts up as a filmer on the deck would, eased over the rider's travel.

The building dimensions, annex, fence, gate and houses are visual estimates. Photos remain external references and are not copied into the shipped bundle. Changing rider stance never mirrors the landmark.

## Realistic look (November 2026)

Wallenberg is drawn under the realistic look shared with El Toro, Hollywood High and Carlsbad Gap (`WALLENBERG_LOOK` in `wallenberg3d.ts`, built on `CARLSBAD_LOOK`; `sets/surfaces.ts` lights and textures each face by its surface). The scene is the spot as the 2020 FindSkateSpots photograph and the 2024 group photograph show it. The layout, physics, drop-in and cameras are unchanged (`wallenbergLayout.ts`, `wallenbergCamera.ts`).

- **Paint** (`wallenbergPalette.ts`) is each surface's sunlit colour, read by eye from the photographs, not sampled.
- **The blocks** (`wallenbergGeometry.ts`): asphalt treads, concrete faces and caps, each curb outlined as its own part. The faces' paint is baked into their vertex colours (`faceTone` in `wallenbergWear.ts`): patches of fresher and older buff, scrapes low along the line, grime toward the foot. Decals layered on a wall z-fight at a distance and read as grey dashes. Weeds grow in the joints off the line.
- **Wear** (`wallenbergWear.ts`) goes on the decals, 0.3, 0.6 or 0.9 units proud by layer: caps worn pale and waxed dark across the line, a polished lane down each tread, fresher sealer patches, cracks, gum, tyre tracks and oil up the slope, landing scuffs and the storm drain in the yard. A test keeps every level decal between 0.2 and 1 unit above the ground.
- **The gym** (`wallenbergBuildings.ts`): cream painted block (the look's masonry, joints a shade darker) over a common-brick base (the look's brick shader, no per-brick quads). Powder-blue ribbed panels sit under dark glazing between block pilasters, with round vents and wall-pack lights. On the front are the door, blue awning, conduit and copper downspout, and on the side, the little roof the roll-in butts against. Also here: the brick annex with its pink door, the planter of white hydrangeas and the rail on its roof, and stucco apartments with bay windows, garages and cornices up the hill and across the yard.
- **Fences** are drawn per pixel by the wire-mesh overlay (`wireMesh.ts`), so they never sparkle.
- **Trees and cars** (`wallenbergTrees.ts`): dense ficus on the bank past the side stairs and in front of the uphill houses, gums standing over the houses, young street trees across the yard. School vans and a staff car are parked in the level lot beyond the gate, and a hatchback across the yard. A test keeps them off the blocks, the yard in front of them and the gym.
- **Shade**: contact darkening along each curb's foot, the gym and annex walls, the ledge and the side stairs. Shadow boxes for the gym, the annex, the awning, the door roof and the roll-in. Rail shadows for every galvanized pipe. All of it stays inside the look's shader budgets (tested).
