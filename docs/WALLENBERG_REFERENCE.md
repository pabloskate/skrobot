# Wallenberg four block reference

## Sources inspected

- [Jenkem, *We charted some of skateboarding's most iconic spots* (27 July 2017)](https://www.jenkemmag.com/home/2017/07/27/charted-skateboardings-iconic-spots/). The magazine collected spot measurements from readers. Its Wallenberg entry gives **51 inches high, 198 inches long**. Those inch values are authoritative for this reconstruction; the page rounds 51 inches to 4.3 feet, and other retellings give conflicting heights.
- [FindSkateSpots, Wallenberg 4](https://findskatespots.com/spots/sf-ca/vega-street-wallenberg-4), [photograph inspected](https://images.findskatespots.com/images/large/8a4d5975c64090456b312c9aa5a9c852.jpg). The unobstructed oblique view establishes the four weathered white curb faces, broad **asphalt** treads, white school wall, red brick base, blue window panels, silver pedestrian rails at the far end, and weeds/cracks. The steps are not four solid white platforms.
- [Jenkem, *A new generation's take on Wallenberg* (14 November 2024)](https://www.jenkemmag.com/home/2024/11/14/a-new-generations-take-on-wallenberg/), photographs by Bailey Schreiner; [group photograph inspected](https://cdn.jenkemmag.com/mediaAssetsMaster/2024/11/GROUPFEAT.png). This confirms the building behind the launch, portable plywood roll-in, brick retaining wall, parking area and chain-link gate. Interviewees describe rolling in from the roof and the resurfaced asphalt landing.
- [Lowcard, SF Skate Club at Wallenberg](https://www.lowcardmag.com/news/sf-skate-club-at-wallenberg/). Schoolyard photography provides context for the basketball equipment beyond the main gap.
- The user's [Ryan Ferguson overview](https://ryanferguson.co.uk/blogs/blog/sacred-skate-spots-skateboarding-world) served as the discovery starting point; detailed geometry uses the sources above.

## Geometry decisions

The world uses 29 units per foot, matching El Toro and the rider scale. The first launch lip is `x=0, y=0`; positive x travels down the gap. Four risers fall a total of 123.25 world units over 478.5 world units. Three intervening terraces are 159.5 units (5.5 feet) deep. Equal 12.75-inch risers are an approximation: the overall measurement is reported, but individual curb heights and terrace depths were not surveyed.

The 38-foot block width, building dimensions, ordinary neighboring stairs, court and fence placement are visual estimates. The pale curb caps, worn paint patches, brick courses, window divisions, conduit, lamps, cracks and small weeds reproduce visual characteristics rather than a survey of each object. No invented central handrail crosses the four blocks; the playable obstacle is the gap.

The school façade is set 32 feet behind the lip to fit the game's flat approach playback and orbit camera. This deliberately extends the short real-world run-up. Wallenberg uses only 0.1 seconds of extra approach before the normal crouch/pop, instead of El Toro's 0.6 seconds, reflecting the confined launch area and keeping the rider and camera clear of the building. A portable curved plywood ramp sits beside the rider's line as a visual reference to contemporary sessions; the animation does not claim to simulate its roll-in. The remote court and vegetation complete the schoolyard from orbit views without changing the measured obstacle.

An uphill orbit shortens its lens as necessary to remain in front of the school during the approach. Distance and focal length change together, preserving the rider's framing and target; tripod locations are already clear of the building.

Photos remain external references and are not copied into the shipped bundle. Materials, cracks, fence mesh and architectural details are deterministic geometry with El Toro's weathered daylight treatment. All views share the same world geometry; changing rider stance never mirrors the landmark.
