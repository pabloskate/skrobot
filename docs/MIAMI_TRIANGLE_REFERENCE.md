# Miami Triangle reference notes

Researched October 8, 2026. This set depicts the classic skateable version of
the Miami Triangle at Bayfront Park, downtown Miami: the gap from the tip of the
Challenger Memorial's raised terrace onto the tilted granite triangle at its
foot, or clear over it. It uses the existing animation scale of 29 world units
per foot. It is a photo-, video- and aerial-guided reconstruction, not a survey:
no surveyed drawing of the memorial is reachable, and the numbers below say how
far each one can be trusted.

## The spot

- **Bayfront Park, downtown Miami.** The Challenger Memorial is in the park's south-west corner, beside Biscayne Blvd and Chopin Plaza.
- **The terrace ("the box")** is a raised **equilateral-triangle** terrace about 88 ft a side with a 60° corner, the tower at its middle. It is board-formed cast concrete painted white. Its flat deck is ringed by a **raised rim**, a white curb that curves up out of the deck along its inner side; its top is the box's top edge. Skaters roll across the deck, pop on the flat a few feet short of the rim and ollie over it off the corner tip.
- **The tower** is Noguchi's tetrahelix, a Boerdijk–Coxeter helix of 31 tetrahedra, about 100 ft tall, in white-painted steel tubes. It stands at the terrace centroid, about 51 ft behind the tip.
- **The triangle** is the granite memorial slab: a thick tilted plate whose top face is an equilateral triangle about 8.5 ft a side in plan. Its **apex is high and points back at the terrace tip**. Its wide base edge is low toward the plaza and stands clear of the pavers on a pedestal under the lower half. It is grey speckled granite, polished, with the engraved inscription above.
- **The plaza** is terracotta square pavers laid as a right isosceles triangle. Its long side is the low planter wall, about 1.5 ft high, which runs square to the line; the terrace tip sits at that side's middle. The two short sides are sawtooth stepped planters. Round concrete pots with plants stand along the edges, and the park's south drive and a parking lot lie beyond.
- **Two lawns** flank the terrace inside the planter wall. They mound up to about 3.1 ft over the plaza and carry hedges, red and blue flowering shrubs and a circular garden bed.
- **Surroundings:** Biscayne Blvd runs north–south past the plaza's right-hand stepped side, which is parallel to it. On the north-up Google z20 tile its east kerb is about 100 ft west of the tip, past a broad promenade with royal and coconut palms, and the Metromover's elevated guideway is along its west side. High-rises line the far side, one white with red balcony panels. The park's south drive and car park run along the plaza's left-hand (south) stepped side. The InterContinental's curved tower is to the ESE (placed by eye about 480 ft off; its distance isn't measured) and Biscayne Bay is to the east. A trapeze rig with poles and a net shows in some clips; it was temporary and isn't modelled.
- **It isn't skateable today.** The box top is now covered in turf, and recent footage shows small studs on the platform edges. FindSkateSpots says the gap is no longer skateable and the triangle remains. **The classic bare-concrete version is modelled** (no turf, no studs), as Leap of Faith and Lyon model their open historical configurations.

## Scene and motion contract

`y = 0` is the terrace deck and the slab's apex; the plaza is `y = −5 ft`
(−145 units). `x = 0` is the terrace tip, and **+x runs down the slab's fall
line and out across the plaza**, on a bearing of about 225° (south-west): the
plaza's two stepped sides run due north–south and east–west on the north-up
aerial, and the line is the plaza's axis. (An early estimate said 217°.)
Looking down the line, −z is left (south-east, toward the parking lot) and +z
is right (north-west, toward Biscayne Blvd). The terrace's two faces run back
from the tip at ±30° from −x, so the deck is `|z| ≤ −x·tan30°` wide at `x < 0`.
Rider stance never mirrors the architecture.

Down the middle of the line, in feet from the tip:

| x | What |
| --- | --- |
| < −1.5 | The terrace deck, `y = 0` |
| −4.75 | The pop |
| −1.5 – 0 | The rim: curving up 3 in, then flat to the tip |
| 0 | The tip: a 5¼ ft face down to the plaza |
| 2.0 – 2.6 | The planter wall, 1.5 ft high, running square to the line |
| 5.5 | The slab's apex, `y = 0` |
| 5.5 – 12.9 | The slab's top face, falling 3.25 ft to a low edge 1.75 ft over the plaza |
| 8.7 | Where the bank line touches down on the slab |
| 12.9 | The slab's low edge, which the bank line rolls off |
| 16.4 | Where the over line touches down |

Both lines are straight, down the slab's fall line from the tip (lane `z = 0`).
They **pop on the flat deck 4¾ ft behind the tip**, about 3 ft short of where
the rim starts on the line, not at the very tip: the rim is raised, so no one
can roll onto it and pop. The takeoff is set with a straight `entry`
(`{ x: −4.75 ft, z: 0, yaw: 0 }`). A nollie or fakie pops off the leading end,
which drags a moment before it lifts, so the pop has to be far enough back
for even that end to rise over the rim. The slab's slope is one-dimensional
along +x, which is why the fall line is the x axis.

**Both lines take a minimum pop** (`DropTerrain.pop`, as a style's
popHeight). From the deck the rider ollies up over the rim and out over the
slab's apex, which is level with the deck. The rim is the tighter constraint:
the pop has to lift every flip and spin, with either stance and the realistic
skater, at least a tenth of a foot over it. That takes 1.05 onto the slab and
1.2 over it. One notch is added for margin, giving **1.1 and 1.25**. The apex
is then passed with about a foot of board clearance, the legs higher still.
Riders who pop higher on flatground pop higher here too. These are playable
trick-clearance choices, not measurements of anyone's ollie.

The **bank line** (explorer: "Bank", the default line, id `stairs`; Dream
Tricks: "Gap to bank") pops off the deck, clears the planter wall and lands on
the slab 3.2 ft down its face, uphill truck first, with the board and rider
aligned to the slab's grade. It then rolls down and **off the raised low edge**,
dropping 1.75 ft to the pavers. The engine's roll-off-a-ledge support covers
this: a roll-out that leaves a raised edge goes airborne, lands once and logs a
ledge, with its own knee sink and dust puff, and the board tips its nose down
off the edge until both trucks are on the pavers. The default line's id is
`stairs` because the explorer's default state and the stage planner hard-code
it.

The **over line** (explorer: "Over", `obstacle=triangle`; Dream Tricks: "Over
the triangle") carries the rider over the wall and the whole triangle to touch
down 3.5 ft past the low edge, and rolls away straight down the plaza.

### Grinding the edges

Grinds and slides go down one of the slab's two upper edges (explorer: grinds
mode, no rail picker; Dream Tricks: "Down an edge"). The user's reference
clips are Jamie Foy (*In the Meantime*, Ride Channel, 2015) and Chad Muska
(*This Is My Element*, Element, 2002). Both roll along the deck beside its
side, pop off near the tip, come down on the far edge a little below the apex
and grind it to the low corner. Foy rides a 50-50 or 5-0; Muska rides a smith
with the nose dipped outside the edge. Both roll away across the plaza.

- **The edges** run from the apex (`x = 5.5 ft`) to the low corners
  (`x = 12.9 ft, z = ±4.25 ft`), 30° either side of the line, falling 3¼ ft
  over 8½ (about 21°). Each is a `Handrail` in its own frame
  (`MIAMI_LEDGES`, `miamiLedge` in `miamiLayout.ts`). `s` runs along the edge
  from the apex and `z` across it, and the stage lays that frame onto the set
  at the edge's heading (`onLedge` in `stage/downhill.ts`). The edges are cut
  plumb, and the face behind each falls away from it at about 12° across.
- **Which edge** is the trick's. The rider comes in from outside the edge with
  the slab on the far side, so the edge is the one the approach the trick
  names puts on that side. A regular rider's frontside 50-50 takes the left
  edge; backside, goofy or fakie takes the right. A boardslide is named for
  its approach like every other slide, here and on every rail: a frontside
  boardslide comes in toeside onto the same edge as a frontside bluntslide or
  noseslide. That's the mirror of El
  Toro's side rails, where the rider comes in from the stairs' middle
  (`ledgeFor` in `sets.ts`).
- **The line in.** The edge's line, run back, passes about 2¾ ft outside the
  terrace's far side, so nobody can ride along it. The rider rolls along the
  deck parallel to the edge, carves in about 22° and pops on the flat
  (`MIAMI_GRIND_POP`, 4½ ft behind the tip, just over the line from the
  edge), clear of the rim. The board turns in the air to line up with the
  edge. The takeoff is fixed (`Handrail.takeoff`): the pop's place and its
  offset from the edge's line.
- **The hop** always comes down with the board's center 1¾ ft down the edge
  (`MIAMI_GRIND_LOCK`), which leaves about 5½ ft to grind. The plain grind
  rolls in at 16 ft/s, the same as the bank line. A higher popper or a trick
  in pops higher and comes in slower to land in the same place, as a skater
  would. A trick in asks for about a third less lift than onto a rail,
  because the gap's hop already hangs longer than flatground. Any less, and a
  360 flip in plays fast enough for its scoop to clip a shin.
- **The locks are ledge locks.** Whatever a lock hangs over the far side rests
  on the granite instead of going through it. The board tips up about the
  edge by the least pitch and roll that clears (`Handrail.ledge`, `ledgeLock`
  in `motion/grind.ts`): a feeble's front wheels sit on the face, a
  boardslide's far truck lifts its end. Blunts have their own ledge stance:
  45° instead of a bar's 35°, with the corner 41 units out along the kick,
  so the back wheels rest on the top and the tail hangs down outside. With
  the bar's contact the wheels went into the granite unless the board
  stood at 63°, which the user found too steep. The snap off the end stops
  where the striking end meets the face (`ledgePopOut`), and lets go of the
  roll as it pops (`ledgeRoll`); otherwise a fakie 50-50's pop clips the
  shin. Known graze: popping off a bluntslide, a thigh brushes the board by
  about one unit for a frame or two. El Toro's bluntslides do it by 3½.
- **Off the end** the rider pops off the low corner, drops about 1¾ ft to
  the plaza and rolls away along the edge's heading, clear of the pots and
  the stepped beds. A slip falls off the outside of the edge onto the plaza.
- **Filmers** move with the edge (`MIAMI_LEDGE_TRIPODS`). The top one stands
  on the other side of the deck from the line in, the side one outside the
  edge, and the bottom one across the line from the ride-away. Otherwise
  they're the gap lines' tripods.

The scenery is the classic bare-concrete configuration. The terrace is white
board-formed concrete with panel joints, form-tie holes, peeling-paint patches
and a raised rim round a worn grey deck; the planter wall is pale concrete;
the lawns carry hedges, flowering shrubs and a circular bed; the slab is polished
speckled granite with engraved names, its top face matching the physics surface
exactly; the plaza is terracotta pavers with stepped planters and round pots;
the tower is a Boerdijk–Coxeter tetrahelix (node `i` at angle `i·131.81°`,
radius 0.5196·a, height `i·a/√10`, with each node joined to the next three; an
edge `a` of about 9.6 ft gives 100 ft over 33 steps). Biscayne Blvd, its palms,
the Metromover guideway, the high-rises and the south lot with cars form the
context. Three tripod views follow the angles in the clips:
- **Bottom:** from the plaza looking back up the line, as Carpenter's filmer did. It stands 9 ft off to the boulevard side, because from dead on at eye level the slab's 5 ft apex hides the deck. From the plaza the deck's edge and rim hide the board on the run-up and the pop; the rider's head shows.
- **Side:** side-on from the plaza's boulevard side, Ehrlund's angle, with the box on the left and the slab on the right.
- **Top:** a filmer on the deck behind the rider. They see the run-up, the pop and the rise. The face falls away at 24°, steeper than their line of sight, so the apex hides the board as it comes down the far side.

`sets/miami/miami.test.ts` checks:
- the documented dimensions and the equilateral slab, apex toward the tip;
- that the slab's slope is the derivative of its ground height;
- one floor at each point, the topmost matching the rider's surface. Under the slab there are two, because the plaza shows through its open sides;
- the rim: raised all round, curving up with no step, and starting 1½ ft behind the tip on the line;
- the run-up and the pop on the flat deck, every wheel short of the rim;
- the bank line landing on the slab uphill truck first, riding the face at its grade, rolling off the lip with exactly one ledge, and ending flat on the plaza;
- board and leg clearance over the rim, the wall and the apex on both lines, for every trick base, stance and rider stance;
- the over line's landing and rollout;
- camera eyes above the surface with the rider in frame, and each tripod's view of the rider clear of the scenery;
- the tower's 31 regular tetrahedra and 100 ft height;
- a finite merged-mesh budget for mobile.

`sets/miami/miamiGrind.test.ts` checks the edge grinds, for every grind and
slide both ways, every stance and both rider stances, plus combos:
- each edge's layout, and that it is a ledge whose top falls away behind it;
- which edge each trick takes, coming in from outside it;
- the roll-in and pop on the flat deck, clear of the rim, at an angle across the tip;
- the lock 1¾ ft down the edge, with the roll-in speed solved for higher pops;
- the lock resting on the edge or the face behind it, never floating, never into the granite, and the board clear of the rim on the way;
- the legs clear of the deck, the rim and the slab, for the robot and the person;
- the board never through a shoe, shin or thigh from the pop to the pop off, for tricks in as well;
- the pop off the low corner onto the plaza and a ride-away clear of the pots and beds;
- the crane and each edge's tripods: in frame, clear of the line in, and seeing the rider past the scenery.

`stage/bank.test.ts` runs its anatomy checks on Miami's bank landing as well as Sunset's. It also checks the roll-off-a-ledge flight itself, and that Sunset's bank never logs a ledge. The sources stay external research references.
