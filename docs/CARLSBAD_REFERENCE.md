# Carlsbad Gap reference

The scene reconstructs the original Carlsbad High School grass gap as it stood
on an ordinary day around 2000–2005, before its February 2012 demolition. It is
not the replacement campus's twelve-stair or Carlsbad's unrelated Water Gap. It
is available in Dream Tricks and the Trick Explorer.

## Sources inspected

- [Chrome Ball Incident #759](https://chromeballincident.blogspot.com/2012/02/chrome-ball-incident-759-legacy-of.html),
  a collection of magazine scans and the sharpest views of the spot. Two
  early-1990s overheads shot from the stair top settle the ground plan: the
  straight toe wall, the curb that leaves its tip and wraps the grass lobe,
  the curved path and its slab joints, and the tall block wall beyond.
  Brandon Turner's colour sequence (about 2000) sets the era's look: deep
  green bank, yellow stair noses, grey rails, and classroom doors 505 and 506
  on the wing beside the grass.
- [Krak's archival montage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A7LnmEIsa84),
  with its [archive and clip credits](https://skatekrak.com/mag/carlsbad).
  Frames between 9 and 59 seconds were inspected for the approach, the rails,
  and the reverse views of the curved landing and the far wall.
- [Wide contest photograph](https://sixby6.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/carlsbad-hot-spots.jpg)
  and [demolition photograph](https://sixby6.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/carlsbad-demolished.jpg),
  from [THE6BY6's archival feature](https://sixby6.wordpress.com/2015/05/13/hot-spots-hammers-at-carlsbad/).
  They establish eleven yellow-edged risers, a rail on each side with none in
  the centre, low concrete cheeks, grass on both sides of the stairs and wide
  concrete at the stair foot.
- [Front photograph](https://fillow.net/images/spots/carlsbad-gap.jpg).
  Inspected for the classroom wall, the two brown doors, the bands of
  horizontal-pane windows, the deep eave and the curled rail returns.
- [J-Ros's original photograph on Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carlsbad_Gap.JPG).
  Inspected for the green bank, the wall cap and the round drain.
- [Transworld's demolition-day photograph](https://www.skateboarding.com/blogs/news/tws-10-top-10-carlsbad-hammers)
  (Skin Phillips, 23 February 2012). Used only for the backdrop: dense tree
  canopy, distant palms.
- [Jeremy Wray's firsthand account](https://www.limitlessculturex.com/post/remembering-the-carlsbad-gap-with-jeremy-wray).
  Confirms the uphill landing, slick pavement and severe impact.
- [Keen Ramps' replica-building account](https://keenramps.com/blogs/posts-without-blog/2018-sun-diego-am-slam-built-by-keen-ramps).
  Describes the curved, uphill landing. A replica is useful corroboration,
  not a dimensional survey of the original.
- [Visit Carlsbad's local skate history](https://visitcarlsbad.com/blog/carlsbads-skateboarding-legacy-2025/).
  Identifies the original eleven-stair gap and its removal.
- USDA NAIP aerials of the campus (2005, 2009, 2010), roughly one metre a
  pixel. The gap is at the south end of the campus: the stairs are a pale
  north-south strip beside the lower half of a long white-roofed classroom
  wing, and the curved path wraps that wing's south-east end. The three years
  agree on the wing's direction to within a degree, so the angle between the
  wing and the stairs is measured from them. Positions read off them are good
  to about eight feet.
- Later photographs from the bank, the landing and the stair head (a 2010s
  contest, and a fisheye of the empty spot). The paint and rails had changed
  by then, but they show the plan plainly: the wing at an angle beside the
  grass, its covered walk running back from the stair-top pad, the wide open
  pad, and the portable classroom beyond the landing.

External photographs and footage remain research references. They are not
shipped as game textures or assets.

## Ground plan

Looking uphill from the landing, as the bottom tripod does: the **stairs are on
the left** (+z), the **grass on the right** (−z), and the **classroom wing
stands beside the grass, at an angle**: 28.5° off the stairs' line, its near
end out past the foot of the bank and its length running back across behind
the stair-top pad. Its covered walk opens onto that pad, and that is where the
speed came from: riders pushed down the walk under the eave and carved out
onto the pad for the lip.

An earlier version of this scene stood the classrooms square-on behind the
lip. The aerials and the photographs from the bank both rule that out.

- **Stairs**: eleven risers between two low concrete cheeks. Each cheek's top
  runs parallel to the stair noses, with a level pedestal at the head and a
  short block at the foot. One galvanised tube rail stands on each cheek, with
  a crook over the top post and an S-curl return at the bottom. Every nose is
  painted safety yellow: the riser almost to its foot and about five inches of
  the tread behind it. From the landing the flight reads as bands of yellow,
  and from the stair head as stripes about as wide as the bare concrete
  between them (the front photograph, the demolition photograph and the
  stair-head fisheye agree). The paint is chipped back to concrete on the
  noses and down the middle, and freshest beside the cheeks.
- **Bank**: one smooth grass surface from the edge of the upper walk down to
  the back of the wall cap. The upper walk's edge is a gentle curve.
- **Toe wall**: straight, from the grass-side cheek foot to a tip a few feet
  right of the riding line, with a flat cap, a round drain near the stair end
  and a concrete gusset where it meets the cheek. The rider clears it.
- **Curb and lobe**: a curb leaves the wall tip at the wall's height, heads
  down the landing, then wraps a low domed lawn and returns, losing height as
  it goes. The grass sits flush behind its cap.
- **Path**: concrete, curving round the lobe, with radial slab joints and one
  arc joint along its middle. A cross walk leaves the stair foot to the left.
  The two merge into the apron the rider lands on, which climbs away from the
  wall.
- **Pad**: wide, level concrete at the top of the stairs, open from the
  wing's walk across to a lawn on the far side of the stairs. Its edge along
  the top of the bank is the lip.
- **Classroom wing**: long and low, about 35 ft across its roof. Doors 505 and
  506 sit a few feet apart with a small wall basin between them, and bands of
  horizontal-pane windows run above a sill line to either side. A deep eave
  on exposed beams, with no posts, covers the walk along the whole wall. The
  curved path below wraps the wing's downhill end.
- **Beyond the landing**: lawn and a row of young trees, then a tall block
  wall across the view, between two portable classrooms: one close on the
  stair side, one farther off past the end of the wing. The portables have
  vertical board siding, a low roof with a dark fascia, and planting strips.
- **Trees**: placed from the aerials. Gums and broadleaf trees stand along
  the perimeter and beyond the wing, broadleaf trees on the lawn beside the
  pad and across it, and palms in the distance.
- **Wear**: the bank is dry and bare where it was used (the crest by the lip
  and the stair head, the landing line, the foot along the wall cap, the
  climb beside the stair cheek) and greener low down and toward the far end
  of the lobe. The paving is paler on the pad and darker and older below,
  with dirt washed off the bank along the wall and curb feet, a stain below
  the drain, cracks, urethane marks along the roll, and pale painted-over
  patches on the toe wall and the lobe's curb.
- **Across the pad**: a large dark-roofed building closes the far side, beyond
  the lawn and a cross walk.

## Estimates and confidence

No surveyed plan or original tape measurement was found. The photographs give
the relationships above with confidence; every number is a modelling estimate
in `carlsbadLayout.ts`, taken from proportions in the photographs (stair
treads, doors and seated people as scale).

| Component | Model estimate |
| --- | --- |
| Upper walk to stair foot | 6 ft down |
| Stair run | 11 ft, ten treads between eleven risers |
| Stair width | 11 ft between 8 in cheeks |
| Riding line to the nearest rail | About 4.7 ft |
| Gap on the riding line, lip to toe wall | 13 ft |
| Toe wall | About 9.3 ft long, 8 in cap, 2.0 ft above the paving at the stairs and 1.9 ft at its tip |
| Curb round the lobe | 8 in cap, 1.9 ft at the wall tip easing to 1.1 ft |
| Curved path | 12 ft wide |
| Uphill paving grade | 6.5%, easing to level farther away |
| Rails | 1.9 in tube, about 34 in above the stair noses |
| Classroom wing | 28.5° off the stairs' line (aerials, ±1°); its wall is 19 ft to the side of the lip, the eave edge 9 ft out from the wall |
| Eave | About 8.4 ft at its edge, rising to the wall; beams clear a rider's head |
| Roll-up | Along the walk 6.5 ft from the wall, a 22 ft carve, the last 9 ft square to the lip |
| Block wall beyond the landing | 66 ft past the lip, 8 ft tall |

Six feet down gives 6.55 in risers. The rider touches down three feet past the
wall; that clearance is a staging choice, separate from the 13 ft span.

The rider comes in as riders did: down the covered walk beside the classroom
wall, leaning into a carve out onto the pad, and square to the lip for the
last few feet. The lean is what the speed and the curve ask for (about 15° at
its tightest), taken from the ankles with the board flat on the ground. The
run-up is a little longer than a straight one needs, so the rider is seen
leaving the walk; `runUp` in `CARLSBAD_TERRAIN` sets how much.

Three things are simplified on purpose:

- **The rider rolls in; they do not push.** The walk is where real riders
  pushed, and there is no pushing animation yet.
- **The ride-away is straight.** Real riders rode away round the curved path;
  ours rolls straight out across the apron.
- **One era.** The grass, paint and wear changed over twenty years. The scene
  shows an ordinary day of about 2000: a bank between the dry one of the
  front photograph and the greener one of the later photographs, worn where
  it was used, and chipped yellow noses. It is not the bare brown bank of the
  2010 contest or the half-demolished site of 2012.

The wing's angle is the firm number. Where it ends, how far the lawn lobe
reaches, and where the portables and the building across the pad stand come
from the aerials and are good to about eight feet. Window counts beyond the
two photographed doors, the trees' sizes and everything beyond the block wall
are visual approximations.

Known differences from the references:

- The wing is about 40 ft shorter than the aerials show, and its far side is
  drawn as paving where the aerials show lawn. Neither can be seen from the
  tripods or the crane.
- The tree line behind the block wall is thinner than in the demolition-day
  photograph, and the young trees on the lawn have flatter crowns than the
  round ones in the stair-head fisheye.
- No tree shades the landing: with the scene's sun, none of the trees the
  aerials show can reach it.
- The scene keeps one fixed daylight. The front photograph's low evening sun,
  warm wall and blue shade are not matched; its materials are.

## Implementation and checks

`packages/animations/src/sets/carlsbad/` owns the layout, baked scenery,
terrain tests and cameras. `carlsbadLayout.ts` holds the plan and the height
of every point of ground (`carlsbadSurface`), which the baked ground mesh and
the landing physics share. `carlsbadGeometry.ts` builds the ground, stairs,
rails, walls and buildings. `carlsbadWear.ts` holds the colours and the wear
painted into the grass and the paving. `carlsbadTrees.ts` places the trees.
`carlsbad3d.ts` picks the tree crowns whose shade falls on paving the cameras
see and turns on the spot's look.

Carlsbad is the first spot drawn with the opt-in daylight look in
`sets/surfaces.ts`: each face is tagged as grass, concrete, glass, painted
wall, metal or block, and the shader draws that material and its shading.
Soft shadows fall on the scenery as well as the ground, creases and wall
feet darken, and tree crowns dapple the ground beneath them. Other spots
render as before until they ask for it.

Wear is vertex paint and shader noise; no textures were added for it. Marks
that lie on the ground's own faces (slab joints, cracks, stains and the stair
paint) are baked as a separate `decals` layer, a hair proud of the paving and
drawn with the ground's material, so tree shade, wall shade and the rider's
shadow cross them as they cross the ground underneath. The layer is an
optional part of what a classic spot's `build` returns. Urethane marks, the
softer sun edge and the deeper shade at wall feet are opt-in keys of the look
(`marks`, `penumbra`, `boxContact`).

The uphill surface takes part in landing and roll-out: the board settles onto
its grade and loses speed. The rails are scenery; the grass gap is the
playable line. Both explorers keep the existing tricks, stances, outcomes,
share URLs and video export.

A run-up that carves in off the line is an opt-in of the shared drop terrain
(`carveIn` in `sets/elToro/stairs.ts`); the pop, flight and landing are the
same as a straight run-up's. Shadow-casting boxes may stand at an angle in
the look, which is how the wing and its eave shade the walk.

Tests check the eleven drawn risers; that the drawn ground matches the
physical surface, with no triangle stretched off it; that the roll-up starts
under the eave beside the wall, comes out onto the pad and meets the lip
square, on level paving with nothing in its way; that the cheeks never dip below a tread and the posts stand
on them; the wall and curb heights, the level cap and the grass flush behind
it; a paved riding line from the wall to the end of the roll-out; uphill
wheel contact and deceleration across all four trick stances and both rider
stances; the yellow on every nose, down the riser and back along the tread;
that the ground marks lie just proud of the paving they are drawn on and are
drawn as ground; that every tree stands on the ground, off the paving, clear
of the roofs and the rider's line, and hides nothing from the tripods;
closed, finite geometry within the mobile vertex,
rail and shadow budgets; and framing from all three tripods and the crane in
portrait and landscape. The shared classic-spot clearance sweep also includes Carlsbad.
Browser checks cover the real 3D canvas in both explorers.
