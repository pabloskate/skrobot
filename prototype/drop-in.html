Drop In

The robot stands on a quarter pipe, tips over the coping, and rides the ramp under real gravity. It's fastest at the bottom and carries that speed up the far wall and onto the deck. The game's letters slam in as stickers when it hits the bottom. Then the screen says who sets first and what to do.

What changed

  1. It drops in instead of sliding in. Only the push is scripted. After that it's a small physics sim: speed comes from height, the robot squashes in proportion to the force at the bottom of the transition, and friction is tuned so it crests the far coping with just enough speed to roll out and stop on the deck.
  2. The camera follows. The world pans on a damped spring, so the far ramp comes into frame as the robot gets there.
  3. Same look as Throw Down: purple grip-tape backdrop, concrete tinted with the robot's color, steel coping, sticker tiles, masking tape, and slam type. The screen wipes up from the bottom along the coping's tilt, picking up where the toss left off.
  4. The letters are the title. The game's letters slam in as stickers the moment the robot bottoms out, and a strip of tape adds “Let's”. An S.K.8 game gets three tiles.
  5. It says what to do next. “You set first. Land any trick. Sparky has to match it.”
  6. The robot reacts with poses it already has. It hops into stoked when it sets first and settles idle when you do. It gets no new body parts.
  7. Tap anywhere to skip. At the end it fades into the game underneath instead of cutting to it.

How it maps to the code

This replaces the inside of GameStartAnimation and keeps its props and contract. The motion is a small pure module that can be tested.

Keeps

  • Props: robot, playerFirst, onComplete. It still mounts from RpsPanel when you press Start.
  • RobotAvatar poses: idle and stoked only, with the same drawing the rest of the app uses.
  • Engine: the START dispatch is unchanged, so the game's own “You won the toss” note still greets you underneath.

Adds

  • dropIn.ts: ramp geometry plus a fixed-step integrator that returns a timeline (position, speed, camera). Tests can pin “crests the far coping, never rolls back, stops on the deck, finishes under 3.5 s”.
  • One SVG plus requestAnimationFrame, transforms only. The type and stickers are CSS.
  • A letters prop from lettersForFormat, so S.K.8 games get three tiles.
  • Tap to skip, and reduced motion shows the settled frame for 1.2 s, then fades.
  • Layout: the ramp scene anchors to the bottom and scales to the screen width, and the text rows are anchored top and bottom.
